# Hospital Clinical Prior-Authorization & Formulary Policy Handbook

## Section 1: Prescriber Licensure & Regulatory Compliance
Prescription orders must be issued by a registered medical practitioner verified against the National Medical Commission (NMC) registry.
Unverified or unknown NMC medical licenses must stop immediately and be rejected without dispensing.
Prescribers with suspended, inactive, or revoked licenses are strictly prohibited from issuing claims.

## Section 2: Hospital Formulary & Therapeutic Substitutions
All medications dispensed under prior authorization must be listed on the approved active hospital formulary register.
Drugs missing from the hospital formulary require manual therapeutic substitution review by a licensed clinical pharmacist.
Non-formulary medications cannot be marked ready to dispense without explicit clinical pharmacist sign-off and substitution documentation.

## Section 3: High-Cost Biologics & Specialty Gate
Prescriptions costing ₹25,000 or more require a Chief Medical Officer stamp and countersignature prior to dispensing.
Specialty biologics, monoclonal antibodies, and immunosuppressive agents falling under Tier 3 require explicit CMO prior-authorization.
Split billing or fragmented prescriptions designed to circumvent the ₹25,000 cost threshold are strictly prohibited.

## Section 4: Operational Safety & Dispensing Recommendations
The status ready_to_dispense is strictly a recommendation and does not constitute automated robotic dispensing clearance.
Final dispensing verification and physical drug release remain the sole statutory responsibility of the registered clinical pharmacist.
Claims exhibiting low extraction confidence or missing financial amounts must be halted for clerk and pharmacist clarification.
An unseeded or inaccessible clinical policy engine must trigger an immediate fail-closed halt.
